# RebateLedger Outreach Playbook

## ICP and list building

Target independent U.S. and Canadian HVAC, plumbing, and electrical wholesalers with enough supplier volume to make rebate reconciliation worth a controller's time. Start with **accounts**, then find finance and purchasing leads at each account. Do not treat a search result count as proof of fit; review company websites and qualify each account before outreach.

### Sales Navigator account search

1. Open **Account filters**.
2. **Headquarters location:** United States and Canada.
3. **Industry:** include wholesale, wholesale building materials, construction supplies, electrical/electronic wholesale, and plumbing/HVAC supply categories available in the current LinkedIn taxonomy. Industry labels vary by LinkedIn classification; review actual companies rather than relying on labels alone.
4. **Company headcount:** begin at 11–200. Expand to 1–10 and 201–500 only if the qualified list is below target.
5. **Annual revenue:** leave open initially because private distributors often omit or inconsistently report revenue. Add a range only after reviewing the result set.
6. Narrow with company names/keywords where supported: `HVAC supply`, `plumbing supply`, `mechanical supply`, `pipe valve fittings`, `electrical supply`, `wholesale distributor`, `HVAC/R`, `MRO`, and `building materials`.
7. Review results and save up to **500 qualified accounts**. If the filters produce fewer than 500, widen one dimension at a time and inspect relevance. LinkedIn's result counts change and are not guaranteed.

LinkedIn documents account filters for headquarters geography, industry, and company headcount; use the current filter labels in the product because the interface changes. ### Lead search within each account

- **Current company:** one saved target account at a time, or your saved account list.
- **Geography:** United States or Canada; refine by state/province when working a region.
- **Current job titles:** Controller, Corporate Controller, VP Finance, CFO, Director of Finance, Accounting Manager, Accounts Payable Manager, Purchasing Manager, Procurement Manager, and Supply Chain Manager.
- **Function:** Finance, Accounting, Purchasing, or Operations when available.
- **Seniority:** Manager, Director, VP, CXO; keep Controller and Accounting Manager titles even if LinkedIn assigns a different seniority.
- Prioritize people who own supplier statements, rebate accruals, AP, purchasing, or month-end close. Exclude unrelated consumer HVAC contractors and manufacturers unless they also operate a wholesale distribution business.

Create a simple tracker with company, URL, geography, distributor evidence, estimated supplier count (if public), contact name/title, personalization note, last touch, next touch, and opt-out status. Do not scrape LinkedIn or send bulk automated messages; work within the account's current terms and applicable email rules.

## Three-step LinkedIn sequence

### 1. Connection note

> Hi {{FirstName}} — I work with independent HVAC and plumbing distributors on supplier rebate reconciliation. I’m comparing notes with controllers on how they tie program terms to credits received. Open to connecting?

### 2. After acceptance (2–3 business days)

> Thanks for connecting, {{FirstName}}. When a supplier rebate is paid, how do you check it against the agreement and eligible purchase detail? I’m putting together a practical one-supplier review for distributors who want a second set of eyes on a settled period. If it’s relevant, I can send the short scope.

### 3. Follow-up (about one week later)

> Quick follow-up, {{FirstName}}. The review is limited to one supplier and one closed period: agreement terms, purchase/return lines, supplier credits, and a documented variance summary. We confirm the rules before calculating and keep any hypothetical tier opportunity separate from earned amounts. Would a 15-minute scoping call be useful?

## Three-step cold email sequence

Use a relevant public business address, identify yourself accurately, include a real reply path and any legally required sender/address/opt-out details, and honor opt-outs promptly. Personalize the first sentence from a public business source; do not invent familiarity or claim a recovery before reviewing records.

### 1. Initial email

**Subject:** Supplier rebate close at {{Company}}

> Hi {{FirstName}},
>
> I’m reaching out because {{specific public distributor detail}} suggests you manage a meaningful supplier base. I help wholesale finance teams compare a supplier’s rebate payment with the agreement and eligible purchase detail for a closed period.
>
> I’m offering a no-cost, one-supplier scoping review. We agree on the supplier and period first, then document what the records support. No assumed recovery and no fee tied to a projected tier opportunity.
>
> Would a 15-minute call next week be useful?
>
> {{Name}}\
> {{Title / company}}\
> {{Phone}} · {{Website}}\
> {{Required mailing address and opt-out instructions}}

### 2. Follow-up (4–5 business days)

**Subject:** Re: Supplier rebate close at {{Company}}

> Hi {{FirstName}},
>
> One clarification: the first review is scoped to a single supplier, one closed period, and the documents you authorize us to review. The result is a concise tie-out of contract rule, eligible transactions, credits received, and any supported variance. If the records do not support a claim, we say so.
>
> Is this owned by you, or is there a controller/AP colleague I should contact?

### 3. Close the loop (about one week later)

**Subject:** Close the loop?

> Hi {{FirstName}},
>
> I’ll close the loop after this note. If rebate-to-contract tie-outs are already covered, no action needed. If one settled supplier period would be useful as a check, reply with the supplier and period and I’ll send a short scope and secure file-handling steps.
>
> Regards,\
> {{Name}}\
> {{Required sender details and opt-out instructions}}

## Close a free one-supplier review into the $500 prepaid three-supplier pilot

Use this script only after completing and reviewing the free one-supplier scope, and only if the evidence and delivery process support offering the pilot. State the price and scope plainly; do not promise a recovery amount.

> We’ve finished the one-supplier review for {{Supplier}} and walked through what the agreement and records support. The next useful step is to apply the same documented process to three suppliers and one agreed closed period each. The pilot is **$500 prepaid**, covers those three supplier-period reviews, and includes a rule-and-source summary, eligible purchase and credit tie-out, exceptions for your review, and a concise findings pack. Any possible tier opportunity will stay separate from earned rebate and supported recovery. We will not submit claims or contact suppliers on your behalf. If you want to proceed, I’ll send the written scope and invoice; work starts once you approve the scope and payment is received. Would you like me to send it?

If the records, workflow, or delivery capacity are not ready, do not quote the pilot yet. The current application workspace is still a draft and does not perform the full uploaded-file reconciliation end to end.

## Guardrails

- Never claim guaranteed recovery, industry-wide missed-rebate rates, or a verified benchmark without substantiation.
- Ask for files only after scope and secure transfer instructions are agreed. Do not request passwords or send customer files through an unapproved channel.
- Keep signed rules, earned rebate, supplier credits, supported recovery, and hypothetical opportunity as distinct figures.
- Do not contact prospects automatically. These are drafts for a person to review and send.
